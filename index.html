What is Flux-T7?

Flux-T7 is a combination of hardware sensors and a visualization tool. It was designed specifically for understand airflows around the car and inside the engine bay. It makes aerodynamics, airflow, and cooling analysis available to amateurs and semi-professional teams.

Want to understand efficiency of your radiators around the track? Or improve fuel economy by streamlining airflow? Well now it's possible without either paying for an wind tunnel or sticking pieces of cotton to your car.

Who is it for?

Amateurs tuning their ride

Semi-professionals that balance the price of reserving a wind tunnel compared to just slapping a bunch of sensors to the car and going for a drive

Professional teams that prefer making quick and small adjustments first and verifying results and only then spending precious time going into a wind tunnel

And EVERYBODY that wants to understand the airflow inside the engine bay, because the wind tunnel will not show that

What does it do?

The potential is high and underdeveloped

Air pressure

Measuring absolute pressure at multiple points; understanding air pressure behavior during a drive on an actual track in actual weather. Are you wasting horsepower on pushing air?

Temperature

Measuring temperature at multiple points. How much is your turbo heating up your intake air? Do the radiators do good job? Don't guess, just measure them.

OBD data tracking

Data purely from sensors would lack context. Connect to your ECU and track vehicle speed, throttle pedal position, and other data.

How did I come up with it?

As an owner of a Lotus Seven replica, a car known to have the worst aerodynamic drag coefficient of all serires production cars, I've always wondered if there was anything I could do about it. As a hobbyist, renting out a wind tunnel or drawing the car as a 3D model and applying advanced fluid dynamics software seemed out of reach. Designing custom hardware and writing software to help me understand pressure points was a fun idea!

Furthermore, the car having such a narrow front means that there isn't much space for radiators. But there are three: water, oil, and an intercooler. They are placed one after another, just so that they fit. They overlap nearly totally. But nobody has measured which radiator needs cooling the most, and which one lacks the necessary airflow to perform well. And even if they have to overlap, we can still at least make decisions in which order they should be placed. Assuming they all get enough airflow, you may want to place the hottest one the last to avoid transferring heat to the other.

Now we can track where air gets stuck, where it flows fast, and what effects it has on cooling.
